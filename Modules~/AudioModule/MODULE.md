# Audio

## Purpose
Plays the game's sound - effects, music with crossfade, 3D sound at a point - from sound banks the modules that play them own, and keeps the player's music and sound settings.

## Concepts
audio, sound, sfx, music, crossfade, mixer, snapshot, ducking, volume, mute, bank, AudioKey, CD_AudioBank, IAudioService

## Decisions
- **Audio knows no game feature.** HitNPoP's Audio module held every key in one `AudioConstants`,
  mapped a gameplay enum to them through a switch, and wired about eleven other modules' signals
  in its own context. Here the module that plays a sound owns it: its keys, its clips, and the step
  that plays them (owner, 2026-09-24).
- **Keys are parts of one partial struct.** A module adds `Scripts/AudioKeys/AudioKey.<Module>.cs`
  with a `Modules.Audio.Shared.asmref` beside it, the trick `FlowModule`'s parts use; the file sits in
  the module and compiles into Audio's Shared assembly, so `AudioKey.` lists every module's keys.
  Not an enum - an enum cannot be split across modules, and Unity stores it as a number. Not a
  plain string - `Play` takes an `AudioKey`, so a typo does not compile. The id,
  `<Module>/<Name>`, says which bank the sound loads from. The `Scripts/Generated/` folder is not
  reused: its asmref points at FlowIoC's core, which cannot see `AudioKey`, and it is machine-written.
- **Banks live in the module that plays them**, at `Resources/Audio/CD_AudioBank.asset`, found by
  `Resources.LoadAll` - no list anywhere names them. A bank is a table of Addressables references,
  so finding it loads no clip; `PreloadAtBoot` or the `LoadBank` step does. A bank row picks its
  key from the declared keys; at start, outside a release build, a key with no row, a row with no
  key and a row with no clip are each reported.
- **The test module's bank is under `Editor/`, its clips are not.** A test module keeps its assets
  under `Editor/`, so `AudioTestModule`'s bank is at `Editor/Resources/Audio`, where
  `Resources.LoadAll` still finds it in the Editor. Its clips stay in `Art/Audio` at the test
  module's root: they are Addressables entries, and Addressables takes no asset from an Editor
  folder. Module Scanner leaves a folder holding an entry where it is.
- **An update of Audio never meets the game's content.** Nothing the game authors sits in this
  folder: keys and banks are in the consumer modules, and the game's own mixer and settings are
  filed on the AudioServiceRoot in its scene, overriding the prefab. The updater's hashes then
  see only the package's own files. What the game's files depend on - `AudioKey`'s name,
  namespace and constructor, the asmref's target `Modules.Audio.Shared`, `CD_AudioBank`'s fields -
  is kept stable; a change to it is a breaking change with `[FormerlySerializedAs]`/`[MovedFrom]`.
- **Addressables directly, not `IAssetService`.** A Service depends on nothing but packages. A
  bank that arrives with an installed module brings clips no Addressables group has heard of, so
  `AudioBankAddressables` gives them an entry in an `Audio` group when the bank is imported - the
  way FlowIoC registers a screen's prefab.
- **The player's two levels are the buses' exposed volumes; snapshots move the groups under
  them.** A snapshot does not reach an exposed parameter, so `MusicBus`/`SfxBus` carry the setting
  and `Music`, `Sfx`, `Ui`, `Ambient` carry `Default`, `Ducked` and `Paused`. Stored in PlayerPrefs
  under `flowioc.audio.*`, the way Haptic keeps its choice.
- **Settings arrive on Incoming.** A settings screen and an ad reach the module through a
  Connector, so `SetMusicEnabled`, `SetSfxVolume`, `Mute`, `Unmute` are signals; the Service's own
  setters dispatch the same signals, so a call and a toggle take one road. Playing is a step, not a
  signal.
- **Mute is `AudioListener.pause`, counted**, so an ad holds the music where it was and two Mutes
  need two Unmutes. It is not stored.
- **A full channel takes its oldest voice by default** and stops it outright; `Reject` drops the
  new sound instead. Music has its own two voices so a crossfade never competes with effects.

## Known gaps
- The button click sound watches the Input System's pointer, or the legacy mouse where only the
  Input Manager is on; touch arrives through the Input System's pointer. It has been pressed with
  a simulated mouse in the Editor, not yet on a phone.
- No sound follows a moving Transform; `PlayAt` plays from a fixed point.
- No pitch variation and no per-call volume.
- A bank still loading cannot be unloaded; `UnloadBank` reports it and leaves the bank.
- Removing the Audio module leaves every consumer's `Scripts/AudioKeys/` pointing at an assembly
  that is gone; the delete tool does not unwire them yet.
